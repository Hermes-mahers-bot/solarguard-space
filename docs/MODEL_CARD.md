# SolarGuard Space — model card

Covers the soiling model that runs the dashboard (`backend/sg_soiling.py`), the
label-generating physics model (`ml/physics_soiling.py`), and the two trained ML models
(`ml/train.py`, artifacts in `models/`). Numbers below are read from the files named or
reproduced from live API calls made on 2026-10-07.

---

## 1. Intended use

Decision support for solar-plant operators in Saudi Arabia: estimate the current soiling
loss at a site, project it over a 3-16 day horizon, and decide whether to send a cleaning
crew today. Secondary use: annual what-if comparisons of cleaning schedules.

**Not** for: bankable yield studies, contractual performance guarantees, automated cleaning
dispatch without human review, or any site outside the 12 modelled Saudi locations and their
climate classes.

## 2. Inputs

Two different pipelines feed the two models.

**Runtime soiling model** (`backend/sg_soiling.py`), per day, from Open-Meteo:
- `pm10_ugm3` (or `dust_ugm3`), `wind_max_ms`, `gust_max_ms`, `rh_mean_pct`,
  `irradiation_kwh_m2`, `precip_mm`, `tair_max_c` / `tcell_max_c`, `peak_ghi_wm2`.
- A site record: `lat`, `lon`, `climate` in {west, east, inland, highland} from
  `backend/sg_config.py`.
- Operator inputs: `capacity_kwp`, `cleaning_interval_days` (default 10), `horizon_days`
  (3-16), optional `cost_per_mwp_sar`.

**ML models** (`ml/train.py`), one row per site-day: 29 features
(`models/feature_meta.json`). They are the raw met/aerosol columns plus calendar sin/cos,
causal rolling means/sums/max over 3/7/30/90-day windows, and causal expanding means of
PM10, dust and AOD. "Causal" = value at day t uses only days ≤ t; there is no leakage from
the future. Targets: next-day soiling loss `%` and next-day dust deposition `g/m²/day`.

## 3. Outputs

- Soiling loss `%` per day, dust mass on glass `g/m²` (display only, from
  `mass_from_loss = -9·ln(1-loss_frac)`), effective exposure days, dust-risk band
  (low/moderate/high/severe), performance ratio, energy kept vs lost, lost SAR.
- Cleaning verdict: `clean_now` or `wait`, current loss `%`, 7-day money at risk SAR, crew
  cost SAR, payback days, and a one-line reason.
- Annual comparison across cleaning habits (events/yr, mean loss, energy lost, cleaning
  spend, net cost, water).
- ML: `soiling_loss_pct`, `deposition_g_m2`, cleaning urgency 0-1, days until clean
  recommended, confidence 0.05-0.99, and per-model detail (GBRT and MLP separately).

## 4. How the labels were produced

The ML models have **no measured soiling labels**. They are trained on labels generated by
`ml/physics_soiling.py`, a transparent mass-balance soiling model whose free parameter
(`site_factor`, one scalar per site) is fitted by bisection so that the simulated mean annual
soiling loss **under weekly cleaning** matches a published band:

| Region | Target annual loss | Sites |
|---|---|---|
| west | 15 % | Jeddah, Yanbu, NEOM |
| east | 45 % | Dammam, AlJubail |
| central | 28 % | Riyadh, Shaqra, Sudair |
| north | 24 % | Sakaka |
| northwest | 23 % | AlUla, Tabuk |
| southwest highland | 14 % | Abha |

The fitted factors and achieved losses are in `models/soiling_calibration.json`. Examples:
Dammam `site_factor` 2.2286 → achieved 44.98 %; Jeddah 0.2550 → 15.01 %; Riyadh 0.1968 →
28.02 %. The bisection converged to <0.05 percentage points on every site.

The model's constants (`soiling_calibration.json → constants`): weekly cleaning,
95 % clean efficiency, 5 mm rain reset, 0.35 per-mm partial wash-off, 45 km/h resuspension
threshold, deposition scale 0.08, concentration exponent 1.15, optical coefficient
`K_LOSS = 0.81` per g/m², humidity cementation 0.35 above 40 % RH.

`models/metrics.json → dataset.label_provenance` states the limitation in the artifact
itself: **"physics_soiling.py literature-calibrated labels (NOT measured field soiling)"**.
The measured part of the pipeline is the meteorology (Open-Meteo ERA5, 2019-2025) and the
CAMS aerosol columns (pm10/pm2.5/dust/AOD, 2024-2025 measured; pre-2024 backfilled from a
per-site climatology fitted to that site's own 2024-25 data).

## 5. Calibration evidence

The label model reproduces its published targets by construction (that is what the bisection
solves). `models/metrics.json → calibration`:

- west-coast mean simulated annual loss: **14.98 %** (target 15 %)
- east-coast mean simulated annual loss: **45.00 %** (target 45 %)
- region-wide mean: 25.26 %; non-east-region mean: 21.31 %
- out-of-sample GBRT (leave-one-site-out) predicted annual means: west 20.99 %, east 37.83 %
  — i.e. the ML, on a site it never saw, brackets the physics label rather than agreeing with
  it exactly.

Published daily soiling rates the runtime model anchors to (from `backend/sg_soiling.py`
comments and `docs/FACTS_SHEET.md`): ~0.1-0.5 %/day typical, up to ~2 %/day during dust
events; global multi-site average 0.051 %/day; 0.45 %/day desert benchmark (IEA-PVPS);
0.083-0.36 %/day measured in Morocco.

## 6. Reconciliation: published annual losses vs published daily rates

This is a real tension and it is stated plainly rather than smoothed over.

Published daily soiling rates for arid sites cluster around **0.1-0.5 %/day** (up to ~2 %/day
in dust events). Cleaning weekly means the array sheds its accumulated dust once every seven
days. At 0.5 %/day under weekly cleaning, the mean accumulated loss across a week is on the
order of **1.75 %**, not tens of percent. A rate of 0.1-0.5 %/day **cannot** produce a 45 %
*annual mean* loss with weekly cleaning — to average 45 % loss across the year while cleaning
every week, the array would have to reach roughly 90 % loss by the end of each week, which
implies a rate an order of magnitude above the published daily figures, or a very much longer
cleaning interval than one week.

The two figures in the corpus therefore describe different regimes. The Saudi/east-coast
"~45 % with weekly cleaning" numbers are annual *site* figures from heavily dust-exposed
locations; the 0.1-0.5 %/day numbers are *incremental rates* measured over dry intervals. They
are not interchangeable, and a model cannot hit both with one set of constants.

What this repository actually does:

- **The runtime model is rate-anchored, and does not hit the annual figures.** It sets the
  instantaneous soiling rate per climate (west 0.30, east 0.80, inland 0.50, highland
  0.18 %/day) and lets the annual number fall where the weather puts it. For Dammam that is a
  mean annual loss of **4.61 %** at weekly cleaning — a tenth of the 45 % reference band,
  which is the honest consequence of a physically defensible daily rate. The 45 %/15 %
  figures are still shown to the operator, labelled `literature`, next to the simulated
  numbers, so nobody is misled into thinking the simulation reproduces them.
- **The ML label model is annual-anchored, and does hit them.** Its `site_factor` fits the
  deposition flux so that weekly cleaning yields 45 % at Dammam. That makes the labels
  consistent with the published annual bands, at the cost of a deposition flux well above a
  rate-anchored daily budget: the label model's mean deposition (weekly cleaning, full
  record) is **0.226 g/m²/day at Dammam**, 0.063 at Jeddah and 0.125 at Riyadh — measured by
  running `ml/physics_soiling.simulate_site(..., cleaning_interval_days=7)` over each site's
  series. The runtime model instead spends the same weather at a fixed %/day rate and never
  reaches those annual totals. The two models are therefore not the same model, and the ML
  inherits the annual anchor.

Consequence for a user: the runtime dashboard answers "what is the array losing *today*",
calibrated to daily rates. The ML answers "what does the literature-calibrated annual
picture look like", calibrated to annual bands. When the ML path is re-enabled, the dashboard
will blend it with the rate-anchored physics (`loss = mean of GBRT and MLP` in
`ml/predict.py`), and the two calibrations will disagree by roughly an order of magnitude in
mean annual terms. Reconciliation between the two anchors is the single most important open
modelling question in this project.

## 7. Evaluation

Two splits, both real, both on held-out data. Full per-fold tables are in
`models/metrics.json`; the headline numbers:

**Time split** — latest 20 % of distinct calendar dates held out (cutoff 2024-08-06;
24,528 train / 6,144 test rows):

| Model | Target | MAE | RMSE | R² | Bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GBRT | soiling_loss_pct | 9.532 | 13.601 | 0.636 | -2.99 |
| GBRT | deposition_g_m2_day | 0.0620 | 0.1082 | 0.355 | -0.0198 |
| MLP | soiling_loss_pct | 7.973 | 11.636 | 0.733 | -3.46 |
| MLP | deposition_g_m2_day | 0.0719 | 0.1337 | 0.016 | -0.0394 |

**Leave-one-site-out** — each site held out in turn, trained on the other 11
(30,672 rows):

| Model | Target | MAE | RMSE | R² | Bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GBRT | soiling_loss_pct | 8.621 | 12.541 | 0.594 | +1.58 |
| GBRT | deposition_g_m2_day | 0.0537 | 0.1007 | -0.208 | +0.022 |
| MLP | soiling_loss_pct | 11.175 | 18.533 | 0.113 | -1.26 |
| MLP | deposition_g_m2_day | 0.0485 | 0.0769 | 0.296 | +0.003 |

Per-fold GBRT soiling-loss MAE ranges 3.58 (Yanbu) to 26.97 (Riyadh, R² -1.57). The MLP
collapses on Abha (MAE 48.29, R² -24.77) — held-out-site prediction of a highland site it has
never seen is essentially unlearned. The MLP's strong time-split score (R² 0.733) next to its
weak leave-one-site-out score (R² 0.113) is a textbook sign that it memorises site/time
conditions rather than the soiling process. The GBRT is the more transferable of the two.

These metrics measure agreement with a physics label, not with a plant. They are reported as
such.

## 8. Failure modes

- **Dust storms.** Deposition is modelled super-linearly in PM10 (exponent 1.15 in the label
  model), so storm days dominate both the total mass and any R². A handful of extreme days can
  swing annual figures; the runtime loss curve is additionally clamped at 55 % because
  observations show dust slumps and plants intervene before higher losses persist.
- **Rain wash-off.** Rain ≥ 5 mm removes most accumulated dust (runtime model: exposure ×
  0.15; label model: mass → 0). Partial rain 0.5-5 mm removes `exp(-0.35·P)`. The annual
  estimate deliberately does **not** credit rain (`rain_credited: false`) — conservative for
  Saudi Arabia, but it means rainy months are pessimistically modelled.
- **Cementation.** Dew and high humidity cement dust so wind can no longer remove it. Modelled
  as a humidity multiplier: the label model `1 + 0.35·max(0, RH − 40)/60`; the runtime model
  `0.85 + 0.006·max(0, RH − 25)`. This is a mechanism the literature supports (Al Garni 2022;
  Almarri 2025) but it is not separately measured here.
- **No inverter data.** There is no measured soiling label anywhere in this project. Every ML
  metric is a physics-reproduction score. Wet/robot cleaning schedules, tilt, module type,
  anti-soiling coatings and per-row terrain are all unmodelled.
- **The ML path does not currently load.** `GBRT.from_npz` raises
  `KeyError: 'loss_base'` against `models/gbrt.npz`, so `loss_pct` falls back to the physics
  curve and `/api/version` reports `physics`. See README §ML models.
- **AERONET is a station list only** — level-1.5/2.0 AOD downloads need a free NASA login,
  so AOD "ground truth" is not actually pulled.

## 9. Fairness and limits for small rooftop systems

- The dataset is 12 utility-scale sites (30-1500 MWp). Rooftop arrays are tens of kWp. The
  physics transfers (soiling is per unit area), but the economics do not: the cleaning-cost
  default is 1,800 SAR per **MWp** per pass, and a crew mobilised for a 10 kWp roof costs far
  more per MWp. The dashboard exposes the cost as an operator input (`cost_per_mwp_sar`,
  presets 600 robotic / 1,800 dry Saudi / 5,100 manual IEA) precisely because the default is
  wrong for small systems.
- The verdict rule (`clean_now` when 7-day money at risk > 0.9 × crew cost) is scale-sensitive
  in the same way. At 5,000 kWp and 180,000 SAR of crew cost the payback horizon balloons;
  small operators should set their real per-pass cost before trusting the verdict.
- Tariff is fixed at 0.18 SAR/kWh (Saudi industrial/commercial reference). A rooftop behind a
  residential tariff (0.18 up to 6,000 kWh/month, then 0.30) would change every payback figure.
- `capacity_kwp` defaults to 1,000 kWp when not supplied, and to the catalogue capacity when a
  known site id is given (`_resolve`).
- Water saved is reported at 2,500 L per MWp per **wet** clean, but the default regime is
  dry/waterless. The water-saving figures therefore describe avoided wet cleaning, not avoided
  dry brushing.

## 10. Reproduction

```bash
python3 ml/build_dataset.py            # rebuild data/soiling_dataset.csv (needs network)
python3 ml/physics_soiling.py --calibrate   # re-fit site factors -> models/soiling_calibration.json
python3 ml/train.py                    # retrain -> models/*.npz, models/metrics.json
python3 ml/physics_soiling.py          # print the per-site annual loss table
```
