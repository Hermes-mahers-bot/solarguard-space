The soiling model

Loss grows as an exponential approach to a ceiling, in effective dust days: each day contributes a factor built from airborne dust concentration, wind (deposition rises with wind, then high wind scours the glass), humidity (dew cements particles so wind cannot lift them) and irradiance. Rain above 5 mm washes the array, as does a cleaning event.

The daily soiling rate is the anchor, not the annual headline: published Saudi rates sit around 0.1–0.5 %/day, with dust events well above that. Ask the dashboard and it will show the rate it used, per site, alongside the published annual figures for that climate.

The cleaning decision

For each site we simulate a full year against its own dust seasonality and economics, then score every candidate policy — fixed intervals from 5 to 60 days, plus a sweep of loss thresholds — on total cost: lost generation plus cleaning spend. The winner becomes that site's trigger. The operator's current calendar is one of the candidates, so if adaptive cannot beat it, the dashboard says so.

The models

Two models are trained from scratch in numpy — gradient-boosted trees and a small neural net — on six years of satellite meteorology and dust for twelve Saudi sites. Labels come from the physics model above, not from inverter data we do not have; that limitation is on the model card and in the repository.

The agent

Questions are answered by an agent with ten tools: it can pull a forecast, run the economics, compare sites, check AERONET ground truth, list satellite layers, report feed health, and search the harvested soiling literature. Retrieval is BM25 over the corpus — no embedding key required — so every claim returns with a citation.
